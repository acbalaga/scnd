How SCND works out its numbers

For installers, engineers and careful owners. Every number is worked out in the browser from the owner's own figures and public sun data. Nothing the owner types is sent to a server of ours.

Typical-year sun

Monthly global and diffuse horizontal irradiation come from NASA POWER climatology (2001 to 2020) on a 0.5° grid over the Philippines (748 cells), interpolated to the owner's location rounded to 0.1°. Each month's mean day is integrated hour by hour (Collares-Pereira and Rabl for global, Liu-Jordan for diffuse), isotropic sky, ground albedo 0.2, for the panel tilt and facing. The transposition matches pvlib within 0.07% on eight reference cases. That check shares the hourly apportioning method, so it validates geometry and transposition, not the apportioning model.

Against Global Solar Atlas, NASA POWER is within about ±5% in the wet season and reads up to about 8% low in the dry season. Metro Manila sits on the edge between two of NASA's 1° cells, a sunnier one over Manila Bay and an inland one. SCND uses the inland cell there, which agrees with Global Solar Atlas within 5% in every month.

Performance ratio and bands

PR = inverter kWh ÷ (kWp × plane-of-array irradiation), with bill periods split across calendar months by days. Hybrids use PV yield, not inverter AC output, so battery losses do not read as a PV fault. Green is 0.75 or more. Amber runs down to 0.65 (June to October) or 0.62 (November to May, wider because of the dry-season bias). Red is below that, and a red flag is raised only after two consecutive bills. PR above 0.90 or below 0.30 reads "check numbers", never green or red.

Weather or system

On request, SCND fetches daily irradiation for the bill's own dates from two sources: Himawari satellite data through Open-Meteo, and NASA POWER. Each is compared with its own 2020 to 2025 baseline, so each source's absolute bias cancels, and the two indices are averaged. If they differ by more than 5 points, the weather call is labelled uncertain and the bill stays judged against a typical year. If one source covers under 80% of the days, the other is used alone and labelled inferred. A trusted call judges the bill on weather-adjusted PR. If the adjustment alone pushes PR above 0.90, the weather data is treated as suspect and not used.

Energy split, savings and payback

Self-consumed solar S = G − X (generated minus exported), or G − X − C + D with a battery (charged, discharged). House load = imported + S. Savings = S × the bill's average buying rate (variable charges ÷ kWh bought) + export credit at the bill's generation charge. Export credit above the bill's variable charges is left out and named, because carry-over is not verified. Rates are always read from the owner's own bill. The payback question on the sheet waits for about a year of bills, because a wet-season start always reads behind.

Degradation and battery

Year over year compares weather-adjusted PR for the same calendar month, once bills span 13 months with a weather check. Monthly noise is far larger than a year's degradation, so the sheet raises it only from six same-month pairs. For batteries, monthly discharged ÷ charged is an apparent round-trip efficiency. A drop is flagged when the latest two bills are both 5 or more points below the average of the first three. Grid charging is flagged when the battery took in more than the PV left after export. Curtailment is not modelled, because it needs daily state of charge.

What is sent, and what is not

Bills, kWh and system details stay in the browser. The weather check sends only the location rounded to 0.1° (about 11 km) and a date range to Open-Meteo and NASA POWER, and only when the owner taps it. Saving writes a file to the owner's device. There are no accounts, no database and no cookies.

Limits

SCND is a screening check from monthly totals. It does not diagnose specific faults, see string-level data, model shading or replace an inspection. Satellite sun data can be off by 5 to 10% for one place and month.

For installers

If a client doubts their system, send them the link. SCND gives both of you a neutral reading from the client's own bill and inverter app.

  • It can clear you. The weather check splits a shortfall into the part the weather explains and the part it does not. A cloudy month shows up as weather, not as a fault.
  • Questions, not a verdict. The findings sheet is a one-page list of questions for the installer: fault log, shading check, how the payback was worked out. It never names an installer, and SCND stores no installer names.
  • It asks for what you already have: the design yield estimate and its assumptions, the inverter event log, and the warranty terms.
  • Export credit at the generation charge. The bill decoder explains it from the client's own bill, which helps when a client was told exports earn the retail rate.
