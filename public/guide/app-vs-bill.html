Why your inverter app and your bill do not match

Your inverter app says the panels made 600 kWh. The bill says you exported 360. Nothing is missing: the two count different things.

The app counts everything the panels made

The inverter measures all the electricity the panels produce. Most of it never reaches the utility's meter, because your house uses it first.

The bill counts only what crossed the meter

The utility's meter sees two flows: power you bought from the grid, and power you sent to it. Solar your house used directly does not show on the bill at all.

Putting them together

Made by the panels (app): 600 kWh
Sent to the grid (bill): 360 kWh
Used at home from solar: 600 − 360 = 240 kWh

If the bill also shows 300 kWh bought, the whole house used 300 + 240 = 540 kWh that month.

With a battery it takes two more numbers from the app: energy charged into the battery and energy taken back out. Solar used at home is then made − sent − charged + discharged, so battery losses are not counted as solar you used.

Use the bill's reading dates

Apps usually show calendar months. Bills run from one meter reading to the next, for example 14 March to 13 April. Comparing a calendar month with a bill period gives nonsense, sometimes even negative self-use. Open the app's daily view and add up the days between the two reading dates printed on the bill.

Signs the numbers do not line up

  • Sent to the grid is more than the panels made (on a system without a battery).
  • Self-use comes out below zero.
  • The bill shows 0 kWh sent although net metering is on. The meter may not have been changed yet.

SCND asks for the reading dates and works out the split for each bill.
