Why your solar bill is not zero

You have solar, you send power to the grid, and the bill still is not zero. That is normal. A net-metered bill has three parts, and solar only touches two of them.

1. What you bought from the grid

At night, and whenever the house uses more than the panels make, you buy power. The bill counts these kWh (a kWh is one unit of electricity, what your meter counts) and charges you for each one. The charges that grow with every kWh, such as generation, transmission, distribution and system loss, are the variable charges.

2. The credit for what you sent out

When the panels make more than the house uses, the extra goes to the grid. The utility credits each exported kWh at the generation charge only, not at the full price you pay per kWh. Some guides say exports earn the full retail rate. They do not, and owners who expect that see smaller savings than they were told.

Example (Meralco, September 2026 schedule): the generation charge was about ₱9.70 per kWh, while the all-in price of a kWh you buy is higher, because it also carries transmission, distribution and other per-kWh charges. Your own rates are on your bill and change every month.

3. Fixed charges

Some lines on the bill do not depend on kWh at all, such as metering and supply charges. The export credit offsets the variable charges, not these. So even in a sunny month the bill keeps a floor.

Why solar you use beats solar you export

A kWh your house uses straight from the panels saves you the full price of a bought kWh. A kWh you export earns only the generation charge. That is why moving use to daytime (laundry, aircon pre-cooling, water heating) is worth more than exporting the same power.

When the credit is bigger than the charges

If you export a lot, the credit can be larger than the variable charges. How the extra is carried to later bills depends on your utility, and SCND does not assume it. Ask your utility how it is applied, and keep an eye on it from bill to bill.

No credit before the meter swap

Export credit starts only after the utility approves net metering and installs a bi-directional meter. Approvals can take months. Until then the bill may show no export at all, even though your inverter app shows power going out.
